    - Contractor licensing and bonding requirements

    - Prevailing wage determinations
    - Environmental impact documentation
    
    **Local Requirements:**
    - Building permit documentation packages

    ## Section 2: Subcontractor Onboarding Framework
    
    ### 2.1 The 7-Stage Onboarding Protocol
    
    ConstructCore's proprietary subcontractor onboarding framework reduces time-to-mobilization from an industry average of 23 days to under 7 days through systematic documentation pre-qualification.
    
    **Stage 1: Pre-Qualification Documentation**
    Required from all subcontractors before bid acceptance:
    - Current contractor license (state-specific)
    - Certificate of Insurance (COI) with general contractor as additional insured
    - Workers' compensation policy with experience modification rate (EMR) below 1.0
    - W-9 federal tax form
    - OSHA 300 log (last 3 years)
    - Financial statements or bonding capacity letter
    
    **Stage 2: Safety Prequalification**
    - OSHA 10 or 30 certification for supervisory personnel
    - Company safety program documentation
    - Substance abuse testing policy
    - Emergency contact and incident reporting protocol
    
    **Stage 3: Insurance Verification**
    Minimum insurance requirements for ConstructCore-managed projects:
    - General Liability: $2,000,000 per occurrence / $4,000,000 aggregate
    - Auto Liability: $1,000,000 combined single limit
    - Workers' Compensation: Statutory limits
    - Umbrella/Excess: $5,000,000 per occurrence
    - Professional Liability (design-build): $1,000,000 per claim
    
    **Stage 4: Scope of Work Documentation**
    - Detailed scope of work agreement
    - Schedule of values breakdown
    - Phasing and milestone schedule alignment
    - Material submittal requirements
    - RFI and change order protocols
    
    **Stage 5: Lien Waiver Framework**
    - Preliminary notice requirements by state
    - Conditional waiver upon progress payment
    - Unconditional waiver upon final payment
    - Joint check agreement provisions (if applicable)
    
    **Stage 6: Compliance Monitoring**
    - Monthly insurance certificate renewal tracking
    - OSHA incident reporting integration
    - Safety audit schedule and scoring protocol
    - Subcontractor performance scorecard
    
    **Stage 7: Closeout Documentation**
    - Final lien waiver (unconditional)
    - As-built drawing submissions
    - O&M manual compilation
    - Warranty documentation package
    - Certificate of substantial completion

    ## Section 3: OSHA Compliance Architecture
    
    ### 3.1 OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Core Requirements
    
    Construction employers with one or more employees must maintain compliance with OSHA's construction industry standards. The following represent the highest-citation categories in 2024:
    
    **Top 10 OSHA Construction Citations (2024):**
    
    1. Fall Protection (1926.501) — 6,307 citations
    2. Scaffolding (1926.451) — 3,814 citations
    3. Ladders (1926.1053) — 2,946 citations
    4. Eye and Face Protection (1926.102) — 2,074 citations
    5. Hazard Communication (1910.1200) — 1,947 citations
    6. Head Protection (1926.100) — 1,683 citations
    7. Excavation/Trenching (1926.652) — 1,521 citations
    8. Electrical Wiring (1926.405) — 1,398 citations
    9. Aerial Lifts (1926.453) — 1,204 citations
    10. Machinery Guarding (1910.212) — 1,087 citations
    
    ### 3.2 Required Safety Programs
    
    Every construction firm operating on commercial or public projects must maintain written safety programs for:
    
    - Hazard Communication (HazCom/GHS)
    - Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) assessment
    - Fall Protection Plan
    - Confined Space Entry Program
    - Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) procedures
    - Emergency Action Plan
    - Heat Illness Prevention (state-specific)
    - Silica Exposure Control Plan (post-2017 mandate)
    - Respiratory Protection Program
    
    ### 3.3 OSHA Recordkeeping Requirements
    
    Firms with 11 or more employees must maintain:
    - **OSHA Form 300**: Log of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses
    - **OSHA Form 300A**: Summary of Work-Related Injuries (posted Feb 1 – Apr 30 annually)
    - **OSHA Form 301**: Injury and Illness Incident Report
    
    Electronic submission required for establishments with 100+ employees in high-hazard industries.

    ## Section 4: Real Estate Development Documentation
    
    ### 4.1 Pre-Development Due Diligence Package
    
    Before breaking ground on any commercial real estate development, developers must compile:
    
    **Site Analysis Documentation:**
    - Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ASTM E1527-21)
    - Phase II ESA (if Phase I reveals recognized environmental conditions)
    - Geotechnical investigation report
    - Survey (ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey)
    - Title search and commitment for title insurance
    - Flood zone determination (FEMA FIRM maps)
    
    **Entitlement Documentation:**
    - Zoning analysis and permitted use confirmation
    - Conditional use permit applications
    - Variance applications and hearing documentation
    - Development agreement with municipality
    - Traffic impact analysis
    - Utility capacity confirmations
    
    **Financial Documentation:**
    - Pro forma financial analysis
    - Construction loan term sheet
    - Equity commitment letters
    - Cost segregation study
    - 1031 exchange documentation (if applicable)
    
    ### 4.2 Construction Loan Documentation Package
    
    Lenders require comprehensive documentation packages for construction financing:
    
    **Required for Construction Loan Closing:**
    - Signed AIA Owner-Contractor Agreement
    - Complete construction drawings (100% CD set)
    - Specifications manual
    - Geotechnical report
    - Environmental clearance
    - All permits (building, grading, MEP)
    - Construction budget (detailed line-item)
    - Construction schedule (CPM/Gantt)
    - Contractor qualifications and financial statements
    - Payment and performance bonds

    ## Section 5: Bonding & Surety Documentation
    
    ### 5.1 Understanding Surety Bonds
    
    Construction projects above $150,000 on federal work (Miller Act) and most state/local projects require surety bonds:
    
    **Bid Bond**: Guarantees contractor will execute contract if awarded (typically 5–10% of bid)
    **Performance Bond**: Guarantees project completion per contract terms (100% of contract value)
    **Payment Bond**: Guarantees payment to subcontractors and suppliers (100% of contract value)
    
    ### 5.2 Bonding Capacity Documentation
    
    To maximize bonding capacity, contractors must maintain:
    
    **Financial Documentation:**
    - CPA-prepared financial statements (reviewed or audited preferred)
    - Work-in-progress (WIP) schedule
    - Backlog analysis
    - Banking relationships and line of credit confirmations
    - Equipment schedule and depreciation analysis
    
    **Operational Documentation:**
    - Organizational chart and key personnel resumes
    - Project history (last 3–5 years)
    - Current project list with completion percentages
    - Safety record (EMR from insurance carrier)
    - Bank and trade references
