    ## Chapter Four: Ashfall in the Morning
    
    He found Cai Draven exactly where he expected to find him: at the depot.
    
    The salvage depot at the Bone Fields northern edge was technically a Syndicate operation — a receiving point for the heavier material that unranked scavengers pulled from the deeper ruins, too bulky for the collection point in the market, requiring the larger industrial scales and the reinforced storage bays. In practice it was also a meeting point, a message drop, and the closest thing to a community space that the Scratch had, which meant it accumulated people the way standing water accumulated everything else.
    
    Cai was sitting on a crate outside the depot's main entrance, eating something from a foil packet with the focused appreciation of someone who had not eaten recently and was correcting the situation. He was twenty-four years old, built like someone who had spent several years being repeatedly killed and rebuilt by an ability that did not much care about leaving everything in exactly the right place — which was accurate — and he had the particular quality of stillness that some people developed when they had survived enough situations to have run out of the kind of ambient anxiety that made stillness difficult.
    
    He looked up when Ren arrived. Looked at him for a long moment without saying anything.
    
    "Something happened," Cai said.
    
    It was not a question.
    
    Ren sat down on the adjacent crate. The morning was getting warmer — Ashfall's environmental systems ran on a standard cycle that mimicked a twenty-six-hour day, and the thermal output was climbing toward midday range. Around them, the depot's operations were getting underway: a crew of scavengers heading out, a functionary logging inventory, the particular background noise of a working-class operation that never really stopped.
    
    "I found a gate in the Bone Fields," Ren said.
    
    Cai's eating slowed. "Dead sector."
    
    "Not dead. Sealed. Someone built a chamber under 7-G and left a gate in it. Active. Not catalogued."
    
    "How long has it been there?"
    
    "Two hundred years, approximately."
    
    Cai was quiet. He finished his food, folded the packet carefully, tucked it into a pocket.
    
    "Did you go in," he said.
    
    "Yes."
    
    "Did you come out different."
    
    "Yes."
    
    He looked at Ren with an expression Ren had learned to read over two years of irregular friendship — cross-referencing current situation against memory and finding partial overlap.
    
    "I have a feeling," Cai said carefully, "that I should not be surprised by this."
    
    "My grandmother left instructions. She said to trust you."
    
    "Sera Voss." He said the name like he was testing whether the memory was whole. "She came to me — three years ago? Maybe four. In Outer Reach Station 14. She sat across from me in a food stand and told me that when a man named Ren Voss came into his inheritance, I should be within reach." He frowned. "She bought me a meal and then left. I thought about it on and off. I didn't do anything with it because she didn't tell me to do anything specific."
    
    "She told me to trust you."
    
    "I am broadly trustworthy," Cai agreed. "What do you need?"
    
    "An appraisal, to start. Mira's interface crashed when she tried."
    
    Cai pulled out his wrist display and extended the appraisal function toward Ren. He watched it cycle through: mild curiosity, focused attention, a slight frown, and then the specific stillness that meant something outside his framework.
    
    "Your rank field says ERROR," Cai said.
    
    "I know."
    
    "Your mana field says—" He stopped. Read it again. "Ren."
    
    "I know."
    
    "It says infinity."
    
    "The System capped it. The display can't hold the actual number."
    
    Cai put his interface away slowly, with the careful deliberateness of someone handling something they did not want to drop.
    
    "Abilities?" Cai said.
    
    "Undefined. The System doesn't have a classification for them yet."
    
    "What can you do."
    
    "I can feel mana structure. Everything in range — residue in the walls, gate signatures, the ranked people above us. Without a scanner." He paused. "I can feel something approaching the colony from the outer ring. Large. Moving with direction."
    
    Cai went still. "How large."
    
    "Scale feels like... the Iron Covenant patrol cruiser from last year. The one you could feel three sectors out from the gate-drive emissions."
    
    "That was a cruiser-class vessel."
    
    "Yes."
    
    Cai stood. He was not a person who panicked — Death Loop made panic structurally difficult — but he had rapid recalibration, and he was doing it now.
    
    "We need to move," he said.
    
    "I need to know what we're moving from."
    
    "We can figure that out while moving. More effective use of time."
    
    Ren stood. "My grandmother said someone was watching the gate since it was built."
    
    Cai looked at him. "And the gate opened this morning."
    
    "Yes."
    
    "And now something cruiser-class is inbound."
    
    "Yes."
    
    Cai picked up his travel pack. He always kept one provisioned — he had woken up dead in seven locations that had not been his intended destination.
    
    "You should have led with that," Cai said.
    
    "I was gathering information in order."
    
    "Gather faster." He headed for the depot's side exit. "And start thinking about where Lyra Sol is."
    
    Ren stopped. "Lyra Sol."
    
    "Your grandmother mentioned her too. Said she'd be in the area. A-rank. Probability Eyes. Incognito, probably."
    
    He filed this. A-rank with a named ability — above B-rank threshold, formal System designation. Distinctive power type.
    
    "Why would an A-rank be in Ashfall?"
    
    "Your grandmother seemed to think the area would be relevant," Cai said. "Sera Voss being right about something does not require advance explanation."
    
    They moved.
    
    *End of Chapter Four*
