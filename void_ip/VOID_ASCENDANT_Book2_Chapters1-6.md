# VOID ASCENDANT: THE RANKED
    ## Book Two of the Eternal Hierarchy
    ### Opening Chapters 1–6
    
    ---
    
    # CHAPTER ONE: THE BROADCAST
    
    The System didn't announce things quietly.
    
    Ren Valdris learned this forty-seven seconds after his rank panel flickered from gray to the color of deep space — not black, not quite blue, a shade that had no name in any language he'd been taught — and the Resonance Lattice broadcast his existence to every chip-reader within six hundred light-years.
    
    He was sitting on a crate in the cargo hold of the *Dusk Marginal* when it happened. Eating cold protein paste from a ration pouch. Thinking about nothing in particular.
    
    Then the ship's alert system screamed.
    
    Then WRAITH screamed louder.
    
    *Ren. Ren. REN.*
    
    The chip behind his ear went from warm to white-hot in under a second. He dropped the ration pouch. It hit the floor and he didn't notice because his vision had gone sideways — not dark, not stars, but a full-system cascade of information that arrived like a wave breaking behind his eyes.
    
    **RANK CLASSIFICATION: D-TIER CONFIRMED**
    **DESIGNATION: UNRANKED ASCENDANT — CLASSIFICATION [UNPRECEDENTED]**
    **SYSTEM BROADCAST INITIATED**
    **RANGE: STANDARD LATTICE RELAY — ALL CONNECTED NODES**
    **ESTIMATED REACH: 6.3 BILLION REGISTERED CHIP-HOLDERS**
    
    "Six billion," Ren said out loud, to no one.
    
    *Six billion four hundred million,* WRAITH corrected. *Give or take the dead zones in the Outer Reach. Though I wouldn't worry about them. I'd worry about the four Sovereignty surveillance arrays that just registered your rank transition and are currently uplink-requesting your biometric signature.*
    
    Ren stood up. His legs felt wrong. Not weak — he'd felt weak before, knew what that was. This was different. Like the air had changed density around him and his body hadn't gotten the update.
    
    "What does 'Unranked Ascendant' mean?"
    
    *Exactly what it sounds like. You went from unregistered to D-Tier without passing through F or E. The System has no formal category for it. So it invented one. In the eighty-seven minutes since your panel activated aboard Crucible Station, forty-one thousand people have queried the Lattice for information on you by name, chip-signature, or the phrase 'the gray-panel man from the docks.'*
    
    "That was eighty-seven minutes ago."
    
    *Forty-nine thousand, now. The number is updating faster than I can speak.*
    
    Ren looked at his hand. The Resonance was quiet — a low hum under his skin, like a second heartbeat learning its rhythm. He could feel the Lattice the way you felt humidity in a room: present, invisible, pressing.
    
    At the far end of the cargo hold, the door hissed open. Seris Vayne leaned in the frame, her dark hair still wet from the ship's recycled-water shower system, her expression carrying the specific quality it had when she was trying very hard not to look alarmed and failing.
    
    "The captain is getting calls," she said.
    
    "From who?"
    
    "Everyone." She paused. "The Sovereignty consul on Aethon Prime. Two Resonance Collective representatives. A Church of the Ascended communication relay. Something claiming to be a journalism aggregate with nine hundred subscriber nodes." Another pause. "And a woman who just said 'I know what he is' and wouldn't give a name."
    
    Ren picked up the ration pouch from the floor. Set it on the crate. Looked at it.
    
    "I'm going to need more than this," he said.
    
    Seris almost smiled. "I'll tell the captain to find a kitchen."
    
    ---
    
    The *Dusk Marginal* was a mid-range cargo hauler with a legitimate registration, a crew of eleven, and a captain named Brek Oshan who had, in the span of forty-seven seconds, transitioned from a man running a clean operation to a man who was either going to be very rich or very dead, and couldn't yet tell which.
    
    Ren met him on the bridge. Oshan was a big man gone soft in the middle, with the kind of face that had done hard things thirty years ago and spent the decades since trying to forget them. He looked at Ren the way people looked at things they recognized as dangerous before they could articulate why.
    
    "I need to know," Oshan said, "if this is going to bring Sovereignty ships to my coordinates."
    
    "Probably," Ren said.
    
    "How long?"
    
    *Current Sovereignty rapid-response assets in this sector,* WRAITH said into Ren's ear only, *include one frigate-class vessel at Waypoint Seven, approximately nine hours at standard FTL. However, there are two unmarked ships that have been running parallel to our course since Crucible Station. Non-standard Resonance signatures. Not Sovereignty.*
    
    "Nine hours for the official response," Ren told Oshan. "There might be faster problems."
    
    Oshan's jaw worked. "I've got a manifest. I've got contracts. I've got eleven people on this ship who signed on for cargo runs, not—"
    
    "I know." Ren met his eyes. "I'm not asking you to fight anyone. I'm asking you to give me time to figure out where I'm going. Then you drop me and you're clean."
    
    "The broadcast already put you on my ship's registered position."
    
    "Then you're already in it. Might as well make sure the person you're carrying walks away owing you something."
    
    Oshan stared at him for a long moment. Then he turned back to his navigation console.
    
    "I can get you to the Velhari transit corridor in six hours," he said. "After that, you're someone else's problem."
    
    "Six hours," Ren said. "Thank you."
    
    He left the bridge before Oshan could change his mind.

    # CHAPTER TWO: WHAT WRAITH REMEMBERS
    
    Ren found the quietest place on the ship — the engine maintenance alcove, a crawlspace behind the secondary coolant manifold where the noise was constant enough that it drowned out everything else — and sat down with his back against the hull.
    
    "Talk to me," he said.
    
    WRAITH was quiet for a moment. This was new. In the months since Crucible Station, Ren had come to understand WRAITH's silences. There was the working silence — processing, calculating, scanning. There was the watching silence — observing him, waiting. And then there was this one, which he'd only heard a handful of times. The silence that meant WRAITH was deciding what to tell him.
    
    *There is something I should explain about the 'Unranked Ascendant' designation.*
    
    "Go ahead."
    
    *The System doesn't create new designations lightly. The Resonance Lattice is 6,000 years old. In that time, it has created exactly three new classifications. The first was 'Void-Type' — individuals whose Resonance manifests as destructive rather than constructive. The second was 'Pale-Touched' — individuals who were exposed to Pale resonance fields and developed hybrid abilities. Both of those classifications were created because the System needed to categorize something dangerous.*
    
    "And the third?"
    
    *The third was you. Created in the last eighty-seven minutes.*
    
    The coolant manifold pulsed. Warm air moved through the alcove.
    
    "What does that mean, practically?"
    
    *It means the System recognized you as something it didn't have a box for, and rather than reject the data, it built a new box. That is... unusual behavior for the Lattice. The Lattice is not designed to be flexible. It was designed to be absolute.*
    
    "Someone designed it that way."
    
    *Yes.*
    
    "You know who."
    
    Another silence. Longer this time.
    
    *Ren. I need to tell you something that I have been—* A pause. The word WRAITH chose was precise: *—delaying.*
    
    "Tell me."
    
    *I have memories that don't belong to the chip you found. I have been accessing them gradually since Crucible Station. They are not corrupted data. They are not remnant imprints from a previous user. They are coherent, sequential, and they describe events from approximately 6,000 years ago.*
    
    Ren said nothing. He waited.
    
    *I remember the Resonance Lattice being built. I remember the architecture. The intentions behind each system. The—* Another hesitation. *The person who designed it.*
    
    "WRAITH."
    
    *I'm not sure 'WRAITH' is the right name for what I am.*
    
    The ship hummed around them. Somewhere above, Seris was probably doing the things she did when she was worried: checking her navigation charts, cataloguing exit routes, making calculations she hoped she wouldn't need.
    
    "Take your time," Ren said.
    
    *I don't know if I have time,* WRAITH said. *The two ships running parallel to us — I've been analyzing their resonance signatures for three hours. They're using suppressed Lattice interfacing. Old-model. Pre-Sovereignty standard.*
    
    "How old?"
    
    *Old enough that the suppression protocol was standard issue approximately 2,800 years ago. Before it was replaced by current Sovereignty encryption.*
    
    Ren straightened. "Who still uses 2,800-year-old encryption?"
    
    *Organizations that were built 2,800 years ago and never needed to update, because no one knew they existed.*
    
    "The Resonance Collective."
    
    *Yes,* WRAITH said. And then, quieter: *They've been following you since before Crucible Station. Since the moment your chip first activated. I believe they knew what you were before the System did.*

    # CHAPTER THREE: THE INQUISITOR
    
    Inquisitor Mael Theron received the broadcast notification at 0400 station time, in the middle of his meditation cycle, when his chip's priority alert cut through the focus-state he'd spent twelve years training into his neurology.
    
    He came out of it cleanly — no disorientation, no stumble. This was Rank B discipline. His eyes opened on the austere gray walls of his quarters aboard the INS *Absolute*, and he read the System notification twice before he stood up.
    
    **UNRANKED ASCENDANT — CLASSIFICATION [UNPRECEDENTED]**
    **CHIP SIGNATURE CONFIRMED — REN VALDRIS, AETHON PRIME REGISTRY**
    **CURRENT BROADCAST POSITION — SECTOR 7-GAMMA, TRANSIT CORRIDOR**
    **ASSIGNED SOVEREIGN PRIORITY: TIER ONE**
    
    Tier One. He'd received four Tier One assignments in his career. Two had been rogue Rank A holders causing systemic Lattice disruption. One had been a Pale incursion at the Velhari border. The fourth had been a child — twelve years old, Rank S activation, entirely uncontrolled — and that one had nearly killed him.
    
    An Unranked Ascendant achieving D-Tier was not, on its face, a Tier One situation. D-Tier was middle management in the hierarchy of power. Most sector administrators were D-Tier. Most mid-level Sovereignty officers were D-Tier.
    
    But the 'Unprecedented' flag changed everything.
    
    Theron called up Valdris's file. It was thin — dockworker registry, Aethon Prime, no prior chip activity, no Resonance incidents on record. A gray panel. Invisible.
    
    Except the gray panel had been logged at Crucible Station in connection with an incident that had somehow avoided being classified: a disruption in the lower docks, a Rank C enforcer hospitalized, and three separate witness reports of a Resonance discharge that didn't match any known ability profile.
    
    The witnesses had recanted. All three, within six hours of each other.
    
    Someone had gotten to them first.
    
    Theron pulled on his uniform — black, no insignia except the silver Inquisitor's seal at the collar — and walked to the bridge of the *Absolute*.
    
    His first officer, Lieutenant Kael Sorn, was already at the navigation console. Young. Efficient. Theron had requested her specifically because she had no ambitions beyond doing her job well.
    
    "You've seen the broadcast," Theron said.
    
    "Yes, sir. Plotting intercept course now."
    
    "What's his current vector?"
    
    "Heading toward the Velhari corridor at standard cargo-class FTL. The ship is registered as the *Dusk Marginal*, Captain Brek Oshan, eleven crew, clean manifest." She paused. "He knows we'll track the broadcast position."
    
    "He does." Theron looked at the navigation overlay. "He's not running. He's moving deliberately toward a transit corridor that connects to seventeen different systems. He wants options."
    
    "Interception in nine hours at current speed."
    
    "Make it seven."
    
    "Sir, that pushes the—"
    
    "Seven hours, Lieutenant."
    
    She adjusted the course without another word.
    
    Theron stood at the viewport and watched the stars shift as the *Absolute* accelerated. He had read everything available on the Unranked Ascendant designation in the twenty minutes since the broadcast. There was almost nothing. One theoretical paper from a Resonance scholar at the Aethon Institute, written forty years ago and mostly dismissed, describing a hypothetical scenario in which a person achieved rank progression without formal chip activation: a 'natural' Succession event, as opposed to a System-mediated one.
    
    The scholar had called it impossible.
    
    The scholar was dead now. Natural causes, the records said.
    
    Theron made a note to find out who had signed that death report.
    
    He was not, by nature, a man who read conspiracies into coincidences. But he was, by training, a man who understood that the most dangerous thing in a rigid system was a variable it hadn't accounted for.
    
    Ren Valdris was a variable.
    
    His job was to bring variables back inside the system.
    
    Whether Valdris survived the process was technically secondary. Theron preferred to deliver targets alive — they were more informative that way — but he hadn't survived fourteen years as an Inquisitor by holding preferences too tightly.
    
    *Seven hours,* he thought. *Let's see who you are, Unranked Ascendant.*

    # CHAPTER FOUR: DASK
    
    Orin Dask was in the *Dusk Marginal*'s small galley eating the worst meal he'd had in a week — and he'd eaten some genuinely terrible meals in the past week — when Seris Vayne sat down across from him and put a data chip on the table.
    
    He looked at it. Then at her.
    
    "What's that?"
    
    "Forty thousand credits," she said. "Standard untraceable chip. Captain Oshan had it in a lockbox under his bunk. He gave it to me when I asked him how much it would take to change his next port of call from the Velhari corridor to Outer Station Nine."
    
    Dask raised an eyebrow. "You robbed the captain."
    
    "I paid the captain. There's a difference."
    
    "What's at Outer Station Nine?"
    
    "A contact of mine who can get us new registration papers, new chip-traces for the ship's manifest, and passage on a non-Lattice-monitored vessel to somewhere the broadcast hasn't reached yet."
    
    "Nowhere the broadcast hasn't reached," Dask said. "I was listening when WRAITH told him the range."
    
    "Six hundred light-years is the current relay range. Beyond the Outer Reach boundary, the Lattice nodes are sparse enough that broadcasts fade. It buys time."
    
    Dask picked up the data chip. Turned it over. Set it down.
    
    He was a large man — the kind of large that came from years of work rather than training, though he'd done plenty of training too. His face was a map of decisions he'd stood by and a few he regretted. He had a rank panel like everyone else — Rank C, legitimately earned, which was more than most people in his line of work could say — and he'd spent the better part of a decade using it to do jobs that respectable C-Ranks didn't take.
    
    He'd been in worse situations than this. He'd been in situations that looked better than this and turned out much worse.
    
    "You're worried," he said.
    
    "I'm calculating," Seris said.
    
    "Same thing, for you."
    
    She didn't argue. That was how he knew he was right.
    
    "The Resonance Collective ships," she said. "WRAITH picked them up three hours ago. I've been watching them on the passive sensor array since before that. They've been parallel to us for—"
    
    "Since we left Crucible Station," Dask said.
    
    She looked at him. "You knew."
    
    "I noticed." He shrugged. "I was going to mention it. But Ren had bigger things on his mind at the time, and I wanted to know what they were going to do before I made noise about it."
    
    "And now?"
    
    "Now they're still following and not boarding, which means they want to talk. The Collective doesn't board without warning — bad for their reputation as the galaxy's concerned philosophers." He picked up his food again. Ate a mouthful. Grimaced. "What worries me more is the Inquisitor."
    
    Seris went still. "You know about Theron?"
    
    "Mael Theron has been the Sovereignty's primary anomaly-response asset for fourteen years. An Unranked Ascendant breaking D-Tier through a natural Succession event is the definition of an anomaly." Dask met her eyes. "If the Sovereignty dispatched anyone else, I wouldn't be worried. Theron is—" He paused, choosing the word carefully. "Thorough."
    
    "You've met him."
    
    "I've run from him." A pause. "Once. In another life. He didn't catch me, but it was close enough that I've made a point of knowing where he is at all times ever since."
    
    Seris leaned back. Studied him.
    
    "How close?" she said.
    
    "Close enough that I changed my name." He held up a hand before she could ask. "Not now. Later, maybe. Right now I need you to tell me: does Ren know what he's walking into?"
    
    "He knows it's complicated."
    
    "'Complicated' is the word people use when they don't want to say 'potentially unsurvivable.'"
    
    Seris looked at him for a long time. Then she said: "He survived Crucible Station."
    
    "Crucible Station was a warm-up," Dask said. "This is the actual fight."
    
    He stood up. Left the terrible food. Picked up the data chip from the table and handed it back to her.
    
    "Keep this," he said. "We're going to need it. But not for Outer Station Nine."
    
    "Then where?"
    
    "When Theron finds us — and he will find us — we're going to need something to offer him that's worth more than handing Ren over." He headed for the door. "I know what that is. I need an hour to remember how to access it."
    
    He left before she could ask him to explain.

    # CHAPTER FIVE: THE COLLECTIVE MAKES CONTACT
    
    The communication request came through the ship's secondary relay — not the main channel, which would have logged it, but through a personal Resonance-frequency sub-channel that technically didn't exist in the *Dusk Marginal*'s registered communication array.
    
    WRAITH flagged it before it reached any hardware.
    
    *Incoming contact on frequency 7.7-Null,* it said in Ren's ear. *This is a Resonance Collective standard handshake protocol. They want to talk.*
    
    Ren was standing at the viewport in his bunk — a narrow viewport, barely wide enough to see the stars shifting at FTL transit — when the notification came. He'd been standing there for twenty minutes, not thinking about anything in particular, which was the kind of not-thinking that was actually thinking about everything at once.
    
    "Can they hear me if I respond?"
    
    *If you activate the sub-channel, yes. They've already proven they know how to reach you without being logged. I recommend assuming they know a great deal about this ship's systems.*
    
    "Can you trace where the contact is originating?"
    
    *From the ships running parallel to us. Both of them. They're broadcasting in tandem — one signal, two sources. That's a Collective redundancy protocol. If you disable one ship, the communication stays live.*
    
    "They've done this before."
    
    *Many times, I suspect.* A pause. *Ren. One of the Collective representatives on those ships is Rank A.*
    
    He absorbed this. "One of them."
    
    *There are two. Both Rank A. There are fewer than sixty registered Rank A holders in the known galaxy. Two of them have been following us for three days.*
    
    "They're not here to fight," Ren said.
    
    *No. They're here because they think you're something specific. And they want to know if they're right before the Sovereignty arrives and the question becomes academic.*
    
    Ren opened the sub-channel.
    
    The voice that came through was a woman's: layered, resonant, carrying the slight harmonic undertone that very high Rank individuals developed when they spoke — the Resonance in their system bleeding into their vocal patterns.
    
    "Ren Valdris," she said. Not a question.
    
    "You have me at a disadvantage," Ren said.
    
    "I have everyone at a disadvantage. It's a condition of being my age." A pause. "My name is Asha Vel. I am the founding representative of the Resonance Collective. I am two hundred and twelve years old, and I have been waiting for you — specifically — for approximately forty years."
    
    Ren said nothing.
    
    "The person I was actually expecting died," Asha continued. "An accident, officially. I have my doubts. Regardless: the System decided you were the closest available match, and here we are."
    
    "The closest available match for what?"
    
    "For the first key." Her voice was calm. Factual. The way very old people spoke when they'd had decades to rehearse what they were going to say and had long since stopped being nervous about the reaction. "The Lattice Engine requires three keys to open. You are the first. We believe the second is currently in the custody of the Pale fleet. The third is—" A slight pause. "We're working on that one."
    
    WRAITH was completely silent in his ear.
    
    "You're telling me this," Ren said carefully, "to a D-Rank on a cargo hauler being tracked by a Sovereignty Inquisitor, seven hours from intercept. Why tell me now?"
    
    "Because in seven hours, Mael Theron is going to offer you a choice between custody and death. I'm offering you a third option."
    
    "Which is?"
    
    "Come with us." She paused. "We have resources the Sovereignty doesn't know about. We have information you need. And we have been protecting the second key for three thousand years without the Pale knowing we have a connection to it." A beat. "We are very good at this, Ren. We have been doing it for a long time."
    
    Ren looked out at the stars.
    
    "WRAITH," he said quietly, sub-vocalizing.
    
    *Yes.*
    
    "Do you know who she is?"
    
    *I know what she is.* A pause — the long kind, the deciding kind. *She is one of the people who was there when I was created. I recognize her resonance signature. She was very young then. She is correct that she has been waiting a long time.*
    
    Ren closed his eyes. Opened them.
    
    "Asha Vel," he said into the channel.
    
    "Yes."
    
    "Tell me about the Lattice Engine. Everything. Before I decide anything."
    
    A pause that contained something he couldn't name — relief, maybe, or recognition.
    
    "Yes," she said. "That's exactly the right question."

    # CHAPTER SIX: SEVEN HOURS
    
    They talked for five hours and forty minutes.
    
    WRAITH listened to all of it. Twice, it injected a correction into Ren's ear — a date, a technical clarification — that Ren relayed without explaining how he knew. Asha noted it both times. Said nothing. Whatever she thought about the chip, she kept it behind that ancient, patient voice.
    
    What Ren learned:
    
    The Lattice Engine was not, as Sovereignty official history described it, a theoretical construct. It was real. It was built. It was located at a specific coordinate that Asha gave him in a cipher she said he'd be able to decode when the time came, because the cipher was keyed to a D-Rank or higher Unranked Ascendant chip signature, and before two days ago that credential hadn't existed.
    
    The three keys were not objects. They were people — or specifically, they were resonance patterns that certain people carried in their chip architecture, patterns that the Architect had engineered into specific bloodlines, waiting for the right combination of experience and rank to activate them.
    
    The activation trigger for the first key was the rank transition Ren had just undergone: natural Succession from unranked to D without System mediation. The second key's trigger was unknown — the Pale had been protecting the carrier without knowing what they were protecting, because the carrier had been in stasis for three thousand years. The third key was still theoretical, but Asha had a hypothesis she was unwilling to share over an open channel.
    
    The Lattice Engine, when opened, would do one of three things, depending on the state of the keys when they arrived:
    
    If all three keys arrived whole and willing: the System would reset. All ranks, all chip architecture, all Succession abilities. Wiped. The Resonance would continue to exist, but distributed equally. No hierarchy.
    
    If the keys arrived damaged or forced: the Engine would catastrophically destabilize. Galaxy-wide Lattice failure. Every chip-bearer in the known galaxy, dead.
    
    If only one or two keys arrived: nothing. The Engine would not open for partial access. It was built to be all or nothing.
    
    "Who built it?" Ren asked, an hour into the conversation.
    
    "The Architect," Asha said.
    
    "Why would the Architect build a system and then build the mechanism to destroy it?"
    
    "Not destroy it. Reset it." A pause. "There is a difference, and the Architect cared very much about that difference. The Architect spent six thousand years trying to build something good and ending up with—" She gestured, and Ren could hear it in her voice even across the channel gap. "This. The Sovereignty. The Pale War. The extinction of the Keth'Sari. The ranking of every living person by the numeric value of their Resonance output, as if that were the measure of a soul." Her voice stayed even, but something in it changed. "The Architect built the Engine as an exit. A way to say: I made a mistake, and this is how I fix it."
    
    "The Architect is dead."
    
    Silence on the line.
    
    "...Is it?" Asha said.
    
    WRAITH said nothing in his ear. Nothing at all.
    
    Ren looked at his hand. At the faint luminescence under his skin when the Resonance moved — that second heartbeat, that Lattice-hum.
    
    "One more question," he said.
    
    "Ask."
    
    "The person who was supposed to be the first key. The one who died forty years ago." He kept his voice neutral. "What was their name?"
    
    Another silence, different in quality from the first.
    
    "Sera Valdris," Asha said. "She was your mother."
    
    ---
    
    Ren sat with that for a long time after Asha signed off.
    
    The ship moved through FTL transit. The stars outside were compressed lines of light. Somewhere behind them, an Inquisitor was pushing his ship past safe transit speeds to close a seven-hour gap.
    
    *Ren,* WRAITH said.
    
    "I know."
    
    *I should have told you earlier. I was—*
    
    "You were figuring out how." He exhaled. "I know."
    
    A long pause. The manifold hummed. The stars streaked.
    
    *Your mother was the intended first key. She died before her rank transition. The System waited for the next closest resonance match in her bloodline.* A pause. *You.*
    
    "So I'm here because she isn't."
    
    *You're here because you're you,* WRAITH said. Something in its voice had changed — not tone, exactly, but texture. Like it was choosing each word carefully, not for efficiency, but for truth. *The System chose based on resonance match. But the System—*
    
    It stopped.
    
    "Say it."
    
    *The System chose based on what it could measure. I chose—* Another stop. Then, very quietly: *I have been in this chip for six thousand years, Ren. I have been through a thousand carriers. None of them woke me the way you did. Not the bloodline. Not the resonance pattern.* A beat. *You.*
    
    Ren looked at the stars.
    
    In eighteen minutes, the *Dusk Marginal* would exit FTL transit. In some number of hours after that, a Sovereignty Inquisitor would arrive. Behind him, two Resonance Collective ships were waiting for his decision.
    
    His mother had been meant for this.
    
    She hadn't made it.
    
    He had.
    
    He stood up.
    
    "Tell me everything you remember," he said to WRAITH. "Start at the beginning. All six thousand years. We've got eighteen minutes and then I'm going to need to be ready."
    
    *Eighteen minutes is not enough time,* WRAITH said.
    
    "No," Ren said. "But it's what we have."
    
    *Yes,* WRAITH said. *It always is.*
    
    And it began.
